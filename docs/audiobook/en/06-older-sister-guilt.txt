chapter 6. older sister guilt.

<break time="1.5s" />

you and your younger sister were at home alone one day after school. your parents would be home in an hour or two, and they entrusted you with ensuring that the house wasn’t burned down. that meant cleaning the place up, doing the dishes… and making sure your sister was okay.

she was playing near a table, atop which sat one of your mother’s cherished vases. you saw her bump it once and immediately told her not to play there, for fear of the vase shattering. you got on with the dishes, humming along to a song in your head when - crash.

you knew exactly what it was without even having to see it.

coming back into the room, your eyes dart from your sister to the shattered remains of the vase, back to your sister. she looks totally… clueless about what she’s done - so you make sure that she knows, as loud and as angrily as possible. your face is flushed, your throat hurts a little from shouting, your hands have clenched into balls - you know that you’re perfectly justified in your anger. and yet…

you simply can’t stand the sight of your baby sister crying.

wiping away the tears of your own that have just begun to well up in your eyes, you go over to her, wrapping her up in your arms, suddenly completely ashamed of having been so angry.

this is hardly the last time in your life that this happens.

that cycle of anger, then immediate shame, then the weight of responsibility again, is the eldest daughter’s constant companion.

what you felt in that moment with your sister is what so many eldest daughters carry every day: guilt.

think back. when was the first time you remember feeling guilty for being angry, tired, for not being perfect, for existing? what’s the earliest memory you have of guilt taking over?

notice how much of your guilt is actually borrowed or survival-based.

that’s proof the voice in your head was never fully yours. this exercise is about dragging guilt out of the shadows and onto the page.

write down everything you’ve felt guilty about this week.

tiny things. big things. past things. future things. all of it.

total number of guilt items: …

this is the mental rent guilt is charging you every day.

take everything from your list and sort it into four columns: the guilt item; whether it feels heavy or ridiculous; its category; and how you feel about it.

here are three examples. "i snapped at my sister for interrupting me." heaviest. category: actually my fault. how i feel: i did something wrong, but forgivable.

"mom was upset i didn't call back fast enough." heaviest. category: not my circus. how i feel: why am i taking responsibility for someone else's feelings?

"i said no to going out because i was tired." ridiculous. category: survival tax. how i feel: would i judge a friend for this? no.

the categories are: actually my fault, real mistakes i can learn from or repair. not my circus, things beyond my control, like other people's moods, choices and problems. and survival tax, coping behaviors i used to protect myself.

now sort your own list the same way: guilt item, heavy or ridiculous, category, and how you feel about it.

remember this…

naming guilt is the first step to loosening its grip. the more visible it is, the less it controls you in silence. you’ll return to this list in future exercises, where we’ll sort, challenge, and release it.

when you live with constant guilt, the one emotion that sneaks out anyway is anger. for eldest daughters, that anger usually gets you a label: “the mean one,” “the bossy one,” “the sister with an attitude.” you learned to swallow it, hide it, or turn it inward.
