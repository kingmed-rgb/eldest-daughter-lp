chapter 9. the i don’t need anyone lie.

<break time="1.5s" />

being the eldest daughter meant being responsible for everyone else, even your parents. you were the one making sure siblings did homework, settling arguments, keeping the family running. without you, the whole house might have fallen apart.

that much responsibility so young matured you quickly, but it also carved something deep into you: the belief that you can, and must, do it all yourself. you learned never to ask your siblings for help (what could they do?), and never to lean on your parents (they had their own problems).

the result? you became capable, reliable, indispensable. but also untouchable. even now, asking for help feels wrong, almost impossible. hyper-independence was your superpower as a child. as an adult, it’s the cage that keeps you exhausted and alone.

this chapter is about unlearning the belief that needing others is weakness, and relearning that accepting help is part of being human.

the help script when your brain screams “i’m fine” but you’re not, try these instead:

remember this…

hyper-independence was your shield, but it doesn’t have to be your identity. accepting help doesn’t make you weak, it makes you human. every time you let someone show up for you, you heal the part of you that once had no one. however, when you put down that shield, another survival strategy shows up right away: achievement.

if you can’t rely on others, you prove yourself instead. you earn approval by excelling. you make yourself indispensable through performance. you tie your worth to what you can produce.

this next chapter is about the trap of achievement and perfectionism: how the hustle that once kept you safe has now become the treadmill that never stops.
