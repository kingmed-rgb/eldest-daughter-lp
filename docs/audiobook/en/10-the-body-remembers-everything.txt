✍ affirmations : for your body.

i’m allowed to cancel plans when i feel sick.

i’m allowed to nap in the middle of the day.

i’m allowed to stop scanning the room.

i’m allowed to tell the truth when i’m in pain.

i’m allowed to rest before i collapse.

i’m allowed to stop fixing everyone else’s problems.

i’m allowed to need care instead of always giving it.

i’m allowed to say “no” without explaining.

i’m allowed to stop being the family’s backup parent.

i’m allowed to make mistakes without carrying shame.

i’m allowed to rest even if the house isn’t perfect.

i’m allowed to put down responsibilities that were never mine.

i’m allowed to stop earning love through achievement.

i’m allowed to walk away from family drama that drains me.

i’m allowed to be seen as more than “the strong one.”

step-by-step practices the body check-in.

set three alarms daily. each time, pause and ask:

where am i holding tension? what does my body need? am i breathing?

write one sentence after each check-in.

the tension release ritual.

each night, for 5 minutes: shake your body, groan, sigh, stretch, roll your neck, tell each part: “you can let go now.”

the scan log.

for 3 days, track every time you “scan” other people’s moods. note:

who i scanned. what i predicted. what actually happened. threat level (1–10).

the present moment anchor.

when scanning starts, use your senses:

5 things you can see.

4 you can touch.

3 you can hear.

2 you can smell.

1 you can taste.

the radar vacation.

practice not scanning in low-stakes settings (coffee shop, watching tv). notice how it felt to let go of the radar.

when did you first learn to scan moods, and what did it cost you?

remember this...

you’ve seen how the eldest daughter wound lives in your body: in the clenched jaw, the tight shoulders, the constant scanning for danger. but your body isn’t reacting in a vacuum. it’s reacting to people.

every ache, every tension spike, every held breath usually has a name attached: mom, dad, siblings, friends, partners. the truth is, your relationships are often where the wound shows up the loudest.

this next chapter is about those dynamics: learning to stop parenting your partners, stop mothering your friends, and start building connections where you’re loved, not just needed.
