chapter 13. the wounds we inherit.

<break time="1.5s" />

the eldest daughter wound often begins with your parents.

maybe you were her mirror instead of her child: the one who absorbed her moods, listened to her pain, and made yourself small so she could breathe. maybe you carried your father’s absence, or became the partner he should have been.

maybe a grandmother passed down her own unhealed story, laying it at your feet.

as a child, you didn’t know you had a choice. their wounds became your inheritance.

their needs became your personality. but now, you get to choose again. this chapter isn’t about blaming your parents. it’s about separating what belongs to them from what belongs to you. it’s about breaking the old contracts you never agreed to sign, and re-parenting yourself in the ways you always needed.

the inheritance inventory what i got from her/him (traits, habits, strengths, wounds) what i choose to keep circle only the ones that serve you.

what i got from mom what i got from dad what i choose to keep the age detector after talking with your mom (or dad), jot down:

what age do i feel?

what does my voice sound like?

where do i feel it in my body?

what am i hoping they’ll say?

this helps you notice when your inner child is still in charge - and when the adult you can step back in.

reality statements complete these sentences:

my mother is … and i am ….

she couldn’t … and that wasn’t my fault.

i needed … and she couldn’t give it.

i can love her and ….

her pain is … and my healing is ….

which line felt hardest to finish? which one felt like freedom?

the mirror breaker for 2 minutes a day, look in a mirror and tell yourself:

“i am not my mother’s mirror. i am my own person.”

what shifts when you repeat this daily?

what’s the one thing your parents did that you’re scared you’ll do too?

remember this...

you can love your parents and still heal from them.

you can grieve what you didn’t get without drowning in bitterness.

their wounds don’t have to be your inheritance.

every time you refuse to be their mirror, every time you choose your voice over their expectations, every time you break an old contract, you write a new story. one where the cycle ends with you.

but ending the cycle with your parents is only half the story. because while you were carrying their pain, you were also carrying your siblings. they got cartoons, birthday parties, and carefree afternoons. you got responsibility, vigilance, and a job you never applied for: third parent.

the next chapter is about that hidden cost: naming the resentment, laying down the roles you never chose, and stepping into the role you were always meant to have: not their parent, not their fixer, but simply their sister.
